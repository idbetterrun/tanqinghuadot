no smiting at the diner

want to take over a neutral diner wedged in the cracks between worlds? then give this first-person, deliberately rough-3d comedy management game a go

still in the early stages · from october 2026

heroes, demon lords, overworked ghosts and visiting aliens all need somewhere to eat. your diner sits in the cracks between their worlds, with one house rule: no spells, no fighting. a first-person comedy management game, with deliberately rough, low-poly 3d and a little room for tenderness.

customers rarely tell you exactly what they want. you listen for clues, then answer with a dish: how finely you chop, how hot you cook, what you add and how you plate it. those actions become flavour, doneness and presentation tags. a meal can be literally right and still miss the point — and that misunderstanding might open another story.

the kitchen is only half the job. carry a plate through a crowded dining room while customers wander over to each other's tables, food gets cold and old enemies start glaring. a few words or the right dish can settle a small disagreement; when it turns into a brawl, reach for the fire extinguisher. foam buys a few calm seconds, but the tank runs out and spraying the wrong person costs you their goodwill.

watch who heads for the till and who keeps looking at the door. chasing a customer can mean leaving dinner to burn, and accusing someone who was about to pay damages the relationship. someone who really can't afford the meal gets a different conversation: hear them out, help them save face, and they might stay to wash dishes and become an employee.

each employee brings a useful skill and a troublesome habit. a demon can skip the heating step, but leave a scorched taste; a hero delivers quickly, but may knock someone over; a ghost walks through walls, but sometimes the plate does too. choose a small crew from your roster for each night, balancing wages, fatigue and who gets along. they can misunderstand your instructions or slack off, with warning signs and a chance to put things right.

before opening, your phone holds supplier messages, shift plans and yesterday's takeaway reviews. haggle for up to three rounds, take the current price or order at full price; supplies and upgrades arrive the next day. prepare a few takeaway orders and send them through a portal before the dining room gets busy. their one-line requests offer clues, but the verdict won't arrive until tomorrow.

after closing, count the takings and see what each customer remembered. meals, misunderstandings and small kindnesses change relationships, unlocking stories and special suppliers over later nights. beneath the chaos, there might be a demon lord who only wants ordinary egg fried rice like their mum used to make. each dish is an answer, and your choices leave a trace.

ai interprets requests and instructions, and gives characters their dialogue, reviews and excuses. game rules decide money, success, relationships and which events can happen; character actions stay within a fixed set. without a connection or model quota, the game falls back to written dialogue and fixed behaviours so the core game remains playable.
